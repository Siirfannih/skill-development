# CINEDANCE — BLOCK LIBRARY v1

Working document. You write a short brief and a list of codes — I assemble the full prompt. The full block texts are also here if you want to assemble something yourself or insert a passage manually.

---

## PART 1. HOW TO USE THIS

Send me a message like this:

```
TRICK: one sentence — what the technique is
FORMAT: duration, number of shots, cuts, aspect ratio
ASSETS: @1 = what, @2 = what ... and what NOT to take from each
MAIN: 1–2 rules that cannot be broken
FAIL: what is most likely to go wrong
COUNT: exactly how many of each thing are in frame
TASK: the character's small task that she does not abandon
ACTING: what she is playing internally, in one sentence
BLOCKS: codes from the library
```

You can write roughly and in Russian. I expand everything.

The minimum that works: TRICK + FORMAT + ASSETS + BLOCKS.

## PART 2. EIGHT RULES THAT HOLD EVERYTHING TOGETHER

1. **One trick per video.** The video is built around one technique; everything else serves it. Not "Karima paints in a field", but "the camera is attached to the brush and the world sways around the stationary brush". Without a trick, there will be a beautiful background with nothing happening.

2. **The rule, then its consequences.** Do not describe the picture — give a mechanical rule and derive 3–4 consequences from it. "The device is never visible" → so there is no outside view, no table with a phone, no hand holding a phone.

3. **Set priorities.** State explicitly: "THESE TWO RULES ARE THE MOST IMPORTANT IN THE SHOT". The model will understand what cannot be sacrificed.

4. **Numbers instead of adjectives.** "Beautiful", "slightly", "a little" — the generator cannot execute these. Replace them with a number or a comparison to the human body. Not "a small folder", but "one-twentieth of the frame width / slightly smaller than her head / would fit in her palm" — three independent measurements of the same thing.

5. **Describe the failure.** "IF THE FOLDER IS THE SIZE OF HER TORSO — THE SHOT IS WRONG." Address the failure in advance, rather than after generation.

6. **The negative is the inverse of your own idea.** General hygiene is needed, but half the ban list should concern this specific video: "the brush does not move within the frame", "the world does not stay still while the brush moves", "the folder does not grow when dragged".

7. **Ban contamination from assets.** Not just "do not take the people". Do not take the lighting, depth of field, colour scheme, sheet layout, or objects from the reference frame.

8. **Give the character a small task.** A task she does not abandon during the event. A giant hand lifts the girl — she keeps scrolling on her phone. This makes the character feel more alive than any description of her face.

### CAMERA

#### CAM-HANDHELD — handheld filming that can be felt

CAMERA — HANDHELD, AND IT MUST BE FELT

Every frame is shot by a real person holding a real camera in their hands. No tripod, no gimbal, no stabiliser, no rig, no dolly, no crane, no drone. The handheld character must be clearly and continuously visible — if any second looks locked off or smoothly stabilised, it is wrong.

Baseline, present in every frame:

- Continuous low-frequency wander of roughly 8–14 pixels in both axes, drifting in slow irregular arcs, never repeating and never returning to exactly the same position.
- On top of it, a fine high-frequency tremble of 2–4 pixels at roughly 6–9 Hz — the constant micro-shake of muscle holding weight.
- A slow breathing cycle: a 3–5 pixel vertical rise and fall on roughly a four-second period.
- A slow roll: the horizon drifts one to two degrees off level and back, never sitting perfectly straight.
- The frame never holds still for longer than about half a second before something moves it.

Operator behaviour that must be visible:

- Weight shifts — two or three per shot, each producing a 10–15 pixel lateral drift and a correction back. One is bigger and comes with a slight dip.
- Late reframes — the camera responds a beat AFTER the subject moves, overshoots slightly, pulls back. It never anticipates.
- Footstep bounce — during any walking move, each step gives a small vertical jolt and a brief tilt, so the move carries a walking rhythm rather than a glide.
- Imperfect starts and stops — every move begins with a small hesitation, has an uneven middle with one brief stall, and ends with a settle that takes a moment to stop wobbling.
- Breath-holds — during the most important beat the operator holds their breath: the wander drops to about half amplitude while the fine tremble slightly increases, then a released breath produces a visible small vertical drop.
- Focus that hunts — a human focus puller. Racks arrive a fraction late and make one correction past-and-back, passing through a real transitional beat where nothing is sharp.
- Wind or impact on the camera — two or three sharper 5–8 pixel jolts across the piece.

Forbidden: no zooms, no whip pans, no dolly track, no crane, no orbit, no drone, no digital-stabilisation look, no perfectly smooth arc, no locked-off frame, no snap focus, no camera move that arrives exactly on the beat.

#### CAM-LIVING-TRIPOD — a tripod shot with subtle life

A single fixed camera position, locked, level horizon. The framing does not change — no zoom, no push, no pan, no tilt, no dolly, no crane, no orbit.

The camera is not dead-still but barely alive: a very faint slow organic drift of roughly 2–3 pixels of low-frequency wander with an almost imperceptible micro-tremble, plus a slow breathing rhythm in the vertical. A locked-off tripod shot with a tiny amount of real-world life in it, never handheld, never calling attention to itself. Focus holds and breathes only a hair.

#### CAM-RIG — camera attached to an object

THE RIG — THIS IS THE WHOLE IDEA OF THE SHOT: the camera is CLAMPED PHYSICALLY TO [OBJECT] itself, on a short arm, WIDE-ANGLE LENS, mounted just behind it and looking forward past it. IT MOVES WITH [OBJECT] AS ONE RIGID BODY.

Therefore: [OBJECT] DOES NOT MOVE WITHIN THE FRAME AT ALL. It sits in EXACTLY the same position in the frame for every single frame of the shot — locked, nailed, immobile. The hand gripping it is ALSO LOCKED IN FRAME, entering from the same corner and never shifting.

INSTEAD, THE WORLD MOVES. Every movement of the arm is expressed as the environment SWINGING, SLIDING and RUSHING behind the stationary object. When she moves it across the room, the entire room WHEELS AND RUSHES ACROSS THE FRAME. When she lowers it to a surface, the SURFACE COMES RUSHING UP TO MEET IT from below and stops dead beneath it.

THE LOOK: wide-angle, roughly 18 mm, held very close. The object is HUGE in the near foreground and slightly stretched by the lens. DEEP FOCUS: everything sharp from the near object to the far wall. The opposite of a macro — no shallow depth, no dissolved background, no isolation.

BANS SPECIFIC TO THIS RIG: no object moving within the frame, no object drifting in frame, no object leaving frame, no shifting grip position in frame, no camera following the object from outside, no handheld coverage of the object, no orbiting it, no macro, no shallow depth, no blurred background, no isolated object on a soft background, no telephoto, no static background, no still world behind the object, no world staying still while the object moves.

#### CAM-POV — camera = object

THE ONE ABSOLUTE RULE: NO DEVICE IS EVER VISIBLE IN FRAME.

We are not filming [OBJECT]. We are seeing through one. There is no [OBJECT], no rectangle, no glowing thing anywhere in the picture. Nobody is filmed holding it. There is no third-person angle, no shot from outside. The lens IS the object: it lies where the object lies, it is picked up, it is looked into — and we only ever see the results of that, never the thing itself. If it appears anywhere in the picture, the shot is wrong.

Everything that happens to the object happens to the frame: when it is knocked, the WHOLE FRAME JUDDERS; when it vibrates, the whole view creeps sideways a few millimetres at a time; when it is lifted, the whole world swings in a fast motion-blurred arc and settles.

BANS: no device visible, no product shot, no third-person view, no shot from outside, no wide shot of the space, no cutaway, no reverse angle, no camera leaving the point of view.

#### CAM-PULLBACK-TURN — macro → pull-back → turn to the face

One continuous handheld take in three stages, no cut between them.

**Stage one — macro hold.** The frame is filled by the detail, shallow depth, heavy breath-hold character in the handheld.

**Stage two — the pull-back.** The camera draws back, a short modest retreat with visible walking bounce, revealing in stages: the whole object, then its edges, then its support, then the environment blooming into frame around it in soft bokeh. Focus stays on the near plane; the background resolves out of bokeh as the camera retreats.

**Stage three — the turn.** Without cutting, the camera ROTATES — a pan, the operator pivoting on the spot rather than travelling, swinging off the object and around onto her. Her shoulder comes in, then her arm, then her FACE arrives and the camera settles. The pan is not mechanical: a small hesitation at the start, an accelerating middle, a brief overshoot of a few degrees past her, and a correction back. Focus racks with it through a genuine transitional beat where neither plane is sharp, arriving on her eyes a fraction late and settling with one small hunt.

#### CAM-OVER-SHOULDER — over the shoulder, focus racking into the distance

A reverse angle from directly behind and slightly above her, close in, with the depth order fully legible in one frame: the back of her head and shoulder sharp in the near foreground occupying roughly the left third; the object she is looking at in the middle; and the thing beyond it in the distance.

Held on the near plane first. Then the camera drifts slowly sideways — the operator sidestepping, uneven, with a small pause halfway — while the focus racks off her head and the middle object and back into the distance. The middle object slides toward the frame edge and dissolves into a soft blurred vertical shape. The rack is not instant: there is a real transitional second where nothing is sharp. Her blurred head stays in the corner of frame for the whole beat.

#### CAM-HIGH — high viewpoint

Camera high above, looking steeply down across the whole space, taking in the full geography of the room or the field at once. The figure is small in the middle of all of it. Locked and level, with only the faintest drift. Everything in the space reads as a plan — the relationships between objects are the subject, not any single object.

#### CAM-MANUAL-ZOOM — hand-operated zoom

At a set moment, A HAND-OPERATED ZOOM PUNCHES IN on the detail she is working — the move is fast, slightly uneven, overshoots the intended framing by a few percent and corrects back, then holds. The zoom is mechanical and human, not a smooth digital push, and the handheld tremor increases visibly at the longer focal length.

Normal lens, roughly 45–50° horizontal field of view — natural perspective, no wide-angle stretch, no distortion. Sharp with a natural falloff into the background. Fine organic film grain, mild vignetting.

### WORLD

#### WIND

Strong wind, not a breeze, and every organic surface reacts on its own layer and its own timing.

- Grass is laid over in travelling gusts: waves race across whole fields, flattening blades in a moving band that crosses the frame in under a second, springing back behind.
- Trees bend as whole masses — trunks flexing, canopies shoved sideways and shivering, leaf clusters flipping to show pale undersides in a rippling flicker. Willow and hanging branches whip and lag hard, trailing, never still.
- Bushes shudder at a faster, tighter frequency than the trees.
- Wildflowers whip and nod violently on thin stems, at their own frequency again.
- Loose material rides the air: seed fluff, pollen, torn grass, dust and scattered leaves streaking past close to the lens at real speed.
- Clouds are sheared — edges tearing and re-forming, thin veils pulled off their tops, whole shapes deforming as they move.
- Fabric on the character lags her body by several frames, always.
- Wind noise bleeds into the audio — a constant pressure hiss rising and falling with the gusts.

Nothing gigantic moves quickly. Nothing distant moves at the speed of something near.

#### WATER

The water has a real current, flowing in one consistent direction, and this must be readable: long smooth glassy bands broken by fine ripple texture, with two or three places where the water rises over a submerged stone into a low standing riffle that holds position while the water passes through it. Small debris travels downstream at a consistent speed. Along the near bank the water laps and slides against the edge with a thin travelling line of tiny bubbles.

Across the middle, a broad band of intense glittering specular sparkle — hundreds of small brilliant points appearing and vanishing in fractions of a second as the surface tilts, travelling with the current, the band shifting slowly. Reflections on the surface are broken and trembling, never mirror-clean.

#### RAIN

THE RAIN IS THE DOMINANT ELEMENT OF THIS SHOT, NON-NEGOTIABLE. It falls across the entire location for the entire duration, in five depth zones:

- Directly at the lens — a few large out-of-focus drops streaking past, some striking and running on the glass.
- Near foreground — individual visible drops with real length and direction, lit hard where they cross a light source.
- Mid-ground — a dense readable curtain, drops still individually visible.
- Far ground — a grey haze of falling water softening everything behind it.
- Impact zone — the ground everywhere is wet, running, splashing, throwing up a low mist of bounce spray, with puddles taking rings continuously.

Every surface in the location is wet, streaming and reflective. Cloth is soaked, heavy and clinging in weight, not just damp.

BAN: no rain that exists only as water running down the character's body while the background stays dry. No dry ground. No dry architecture. No rain that stops between shots.

#### LIVING-BACKGROUND

Every layer of the background is alive, and each moves at its own correct speed for its distance.

- Birds: one or two designated events only — a loose group of five or six crossing the middle distance with real wing-beat speed, banking once together and catching a flash of light; or a single larger bird gliding high on flat wings. Small in frame, clearly distant, never crossing in front of the subject. No flock, no swarm.
- Cloud shadows travel slowly across distant slopes, dimming a whole area and releasing it.
- Atmospheric layers shift almost imperceptibly, so the far distance slowly changes density.
- Dust, pollen and small insects drift through any shaft of light and are visibly disturbed into small vortices by movement near them.
- The most distant elements — mountains, horizon, the moon — do not move at all. They are the still anchor in a frame full of motion.

#### INTEGRATION

INTEGRATION LOCK. The character is physically in this place.

- Her cast shadow falls on the real ground and on the objects around her, correctly angled to the fixed key light, and it moves when she moves.
- The ground is compressed, splayed or marked where her weight presses into it, and it stays marked.
- Architecture, foliage and foreground objects OVERLAP HER where the geometry demands it — she is occluded, not pasted in front.
- She is reflected in every reflective surface present.
- Bounce light from the nearest bright surface fills her shadow side.
- Airborne dust, spray, pollen and haze pass in FRONT of her as well as behind.
- She shares the shot's exposure, grain, haze and lens softness at her depth plane.

If any frame shows her sharper, brighter, cleaner or flatter than her depth plane — that frame is wrong.

#### SCALE

SCALE — WORLD AS HUGE. Do not describe her as small. Describe the world as enormous, measured in units of her body.

Give at least THREE independent cross-checks against her body, e.g.: the object is taller than her torso; it is wider than three of her shoulder-widths; its edge sits at her mid-thigh.

Plus ONE physical contact test: where she touches something, describe exactly what happens — her fingers wrap it and the tips do not meet, her palm compresses against it, the surface dents under her weight.

BAN SIZE DRIFT IN BOTH DIRECTIONS: she never becomes larger and never becomes smaller than the locked measurements at any point in the piece.

### RULES

#### PEOPLE-LAW — a global rule overriding assets

THERE IS ONLY ONE PERSON IN THIS ENTIRE PIECE, AND SHE COMES ONLY FROM [asset]. All other references supply framing, staging, location and camera position ONLY. TAKE NO PEOPLE FROM THEM — no faces, no heads, no bodies, no hands, no arms, no fingers, no clothing, no skin. Every hand and every arm seen in the piece is hers.

#### COUNT

CONTINUITY / COUNT LOCK: exactly [N] of [X], exactly [N] of [Y], exactly one of [Z]. These counts hold in every single frame. No duplicates, no second copy appearing in the background, no extra appearing at a cut, no crowd, no swarm, no herd. If more than the stated number is visible at any point, the shot is wrong.

#### FAIL

FAIL CONDITION: IF [the predicted failure] HAPPENS, THE SHOT IS WRONG.

*(write one line for each weak point — size, pose, count, direction of movement, emotion)*

#### NO-FREEZING

NOBODY FREEZES. In every shot there is continuous movement from its first frame to its last — hands, breathing, weight shifting, fabric moving, hair moving. No shot begins or ends on a still figure. No frozen poses, no motionless hands, no dead frames. The last second of the piece has as much life in it as the first.

#### CONTAMINATION — a ban on carrying over elements from assets

For every reference: take ONLY what is named, and take nothing else. Specifically DO NOT inherit from any reference image: its people, its hands, its clothing, its lighting, its colour temperature, its grade, its depth of field, its framing, its aspect ratio, its background, its studio backdrop, its sheet layout, its props, or any object visible in it that is not explicitly requested.

#### TEXT-ON — when text is needed

The text reads exactly "[WORDS]" — [how many] words, [case], correctly and legibly spelled, no other words, no numbers, no extension, no punctuation.

IT SITS [exact position relative to the object], separated by [gap]. It is [weight, colour, case, typeface] with [treatment], sized to match [reference scale] — subordinate to the object, never a headline, never a title card.

IT IS NEVER ON the object, NEVER INSIDE it, NEVER ACROSS its face, NEVER ABOVE it, NEVER BESIDE it, NEVER OVERLAPPING it, and never floating free of it. It travels with the object as one unit.

THIS IS THE ONLY TEXT ANYWHERE IN THE PIECE. No other words, no file names, no captions, no subtitles, no watermarks, no gibberish or misspelled lettering.

#### ANATOMY

ANATOMY — CRITICAL. Every hand has exactly five correct fingers with real knuckles, nails and creases and correct thumb opposition, holding its shape throughout. Two arms, two legs, correct joint directions. No melting, no fusing, no extra digits, no backwards joints, no morphing.

For animals: build the limb as a real skeleton — shoulder, upper limb, elbow, forearm, wrist, paw or hoof — with separate digits, pads and claws where they exist. The limb bends ONLY at real joints. No blob, no rubber, no fused toes, no shapeless mass of fur.

#### RESTRAINED

EMOTIONAL REGISTER — restrained throughout. Every peak reads through the BODY, not the face: a tightened grip, a caught step, a hand on a surface, a held stillness. Calibrated substitutions: a shout becomes a quiet exhale; a scream becomes a closed-mouth flinch; shock becomes a physical recoil that happens BEFORE any expression; delight becomes a two-millimetre brow lift and a slow blink.

Banned completely: wide grins, open mouth, gasps, hands over the mouth, theatrical shock, giggling, tears, trembling lip, eyebrow acting, hair-flip posing, any expression larger than a real person would actually make alone.

#### GAZE

She never looks into the lens — except at the designated moments listed below, which are deliberate events with exact timecodes: [list of moments]. Outside those moments, not for a single frame.

Her head and her eyes always agree: when her head turns, her EYES GO THE SAME WAY. No over-the-shoulder glance into the lens, no head rotation past 90 degrees.

Real gaze behaviour throughout: small involuntary micro-movements, irregular blinks at uneven intervals, smooth tracking when following something, brief downward glances. Never a fixed stare.

### SOUND

#### SOUND

AUDIO — SFX ONLY, NO MUSIC. No music of any kind, no score, no ambient pads, no swells, nothing melodic, no dialogue.

Only real diegetic sound, layered by distance: the room tone or open ambience of this specific space with its correct reverb; the near sounds of the character's body, clothing and contact with objects; the mid sounds of the location; the distant sounds at the edge of audibility. Her breathing present throughout. Every physical impact in the picture has its sound, and no sound exists without a visible cause.

#### SOUND-SWITCH

The soundscape changes perspective with the camera: when the point of view is lifted, moved or turned, the whole sound field swings with it — near sounds recede, new near sounds arrive, the reverb changes. At a hard cut or a wipe, the sound flips instantly and completely to the new space, on the exact frame, with no crossfade.
